Before anything, just let me show you the album cover of the album I've been
listening to most this week. Gotta get it over with.

<Album
  artist="Lift to Experience"
  title="The Texas-Jerusalem Crossroads"
  year={2001}
  cover="/covers/lift-to-experience-the-texas-jerusalem-crossroads.jpg"
  href="https://open.spotify.com/album/062SIuYHTCmS3gAsOupCdA"
  pills={["post rock", "christian rock", "rock opera"]}
>
I mean, with a cover like that, how can you not be intrigued?

Once my eyes healed from the burning, I started up the album with absolutely
no expectations of what it might be. And it's one of those where for multiple
days I didn't listen past the first track because there's so much to take in.

The best way to describe this album is a Christian rock opera, with influences
across most rock subgenres. The trio is from Denton, TX, and these boys love
Jesus first and Texas as a close second. The band, and the album, have grown a
cult following over the years, but sadly remain incredibly niche. It's something
out of a fable: 3 boys from rural TX, and legend has it the lead
singer is 7ft tall.

I also have to say that I never thought in a million years I would be head over
heels for a Christian rock album. It's not just "technically" Christian rock
either. It's full-blown, lyrics-from-the-hymnal,
one-set-of-footprints-in-the-sand type Christian rock, but somehow it works magically.

One of my first thoughts was how amazingly it was produced and mixed. It
reminds me of the production on
*[Grace](https://open.spotify.com/album/6lo0ndbssQu7cPHIYFclvk)*, dare I say
better (not that the album is better, but the production is balancing more
elements). Apparently it was mixed by Robin Guthrie and Simon Raymonde from
[Cocteau Twins](https://open.spotify.com/artist/6X41IADQFJRHZ2m4MPNZRt), so
that makes it make more sense, but also opens another question of how they got
in the studio with Robin and Simon.

I still really like the first track,
**[Just As Was Told](https://open.spotify.com/track/3jynmxddJTt1UQ8RYETT2d)**.
It starts out with a long,
[Slint](https://open.spotify.com/album/2NnkLRaeX33d1Mn8ZLgTo8) inspired spoken
word segment until a train comes screaming through, kicking off instrumentation
that flows in and out of different grooves, like the song has ADHD and can't
stay in one place for more than 20 seconds. I think the song is about an
apocalypse starting? And Texas is Zion? I can't even really tell. There's way
too much going on to focus on the lyrical content.

**[Falling From Cloud 9](https://open.spotify.com/track/7M7lWOjE8rUFVyWFaFsWX8)**
is probably the most accessible song on the album, the best starting point if
you just want to dip your toe in the water. Love it lyrically as well. It
doesn't pretend life is easy, and it's beautiful about it:

> The God-man Christ calls cursed man to the test\
> And this cross crushed into my chest

The imagery points out not only the weight of Christianity and what it means to
wear the cross on a necklace, but also relying on that cross to get through
trials and tribulations. He is the one crushing the cross into his chest,
calling on God to help him through.

Lyrically and vocally it's *very* dramatic, but oddly works for me.

If you were somehow questioning how Christian rock the album is, the second
half will make it very clear.
**[These Are the Days](https://open.spotify.com/track/2zAlnbkJOu5dcUQTEknzFO)**
is the most popular song, and sounds like the most likely to be covered by a
band of youth pastors. I love the concept of "the days" being your trials.

Finally I'll shout out
**[Into the Storm](https://open.spotify.com/track/3yGeKgpndQ8a64mzjMkOek)**, a
metal inspired closer. Don't be discouraged by the 29 minute runtime. The one
problem I have with the album is there's a hidden track bolted onto the end of
it that I think is more of a novelty than additive to the album, and adds 19
mins of near silence to the project.

I'm so excited to have discovered this album, and I couldn't recommend it
enough to just put on some nice headphones and let it spin front to back. It's
so surreal that a Christian rock opera could be something I'm highly
recommending.
</Album>

<Section title="You Love Danger Mouse">

You love [Danger Mouse](https://open.spotify.com/artist/2dBj3prW7gP9bCCOIQeDUf).

Even if you don't know who that is, you do love him. I'm confident in that.

If you were, like me, raised on
**[Crazy](https://open.spotify.com/track/2N5zMZX7YeL1tico8oQxa9)** by Gnarls
Barkley, or love
**[Feel Good Inc.](https://open.spotify.com/track/0d28khcov6AiegSCpG5TuT)** or
**[DARE](https://open.spotify.com/track/4Hff1IjRbLGeLgFgxvHflk)** by Gorillaz,
you love Danger Mouse.

Danger Mouse is Brian Burton, a music producer who Esquire named one of the 75
most influential people (not even just musicians, people) of the 21st century.

He got famous in 2004 when he released
*[The Grey Album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Grey_Album)*, which quickly
got pulled from distribution because of the blatant copyright infringement. The
album, aptly named, is the vocals from Jay-Z's
*[The Black Album](https://open.spotify.com/album/4FWvo9oS4gRgHtAwDwUjiO)*
layered on top of instrumentals from the Beatles'
*[White Album](https://open.spotify.com/album/1klALx0u4AavZNEvC4LrTL)*.

Immediately after getting famous in the music community, he got to work. He
produced the sophomore
*[Demon Days](https://open.spotify.com/album/0bUTHlWbkSQysoM3VsWldT)* by Damon
Albarn's Gorillaz and teamed up with CeeLo Green to form Gnarls Barkley.

He's been collaborating ever since. The criminally underrated project
[Broken Bells](https://open.spotify.com/artist/6dgwEwnK0YtDfS9XhRwBTG) is Brian
plus James Mercer ([The Shins](https://open.spotify.com/artist/4LG4Bs1Gadht7TCrMytQUO)),
and he has produced amazing albums for A$AP Rocky, Red Hot Chili Peppers, The
Black Keys and Adele. He did
[an album](https://open.spotify.com/album/1kgurOfUl0Qu20MDK46xur) with Karen O
from the Yeah Yeah Yeahs and a ton of work with the late hip hop legend
[MF DOOM](https://open.spotify.com/artist/2pAWfrd7WFF3XhVt9GooDL). Suffice it to
say he keeps getting better and better.

[Black Thought](https://open.spotify.com/artist/6DJEUXZm0e2rAohdoZ5Voo) is the
MC from [The Roots](https://open.spotify.com/artist/78xUyw6FkVZrRAtziFdtdu).
You might know him from Jimmy Fallon's late night show. The Roots were a hip
hop group from Philly in the 90's, although the label "hip hop group" feels
wrong with an impact as much as they've had.

Black Thought is widely regarded as one of the most prolific, skilled rappers
of all time. As this new album shows, he also keeps getting better and better.

<Album
  artist="Danger Mouse & Black Thought"
  title="Cheat Codes"
  year={2022}
  cover="/covers/danger-mouse-black-thought-cheat-codes.jpg"
  href="https://open.spotify.com/album/6g8wQSzYvVEOrUc9VqQPBJ"
  pills={["conscious hip hop", "boom bap"]}
>
Cheat Codes is a modern return to 90's style east coast hip hop, that same
decades old structure rebuilt with modern sounds and sampling techniques. In my
opinion it's the best hip hop album of the decade.

Start with
**[No Gold Teeth](https://open.spotify.com/track/3d6qopMExScGOo1RUwgkAq)**. It
was the lead single for the album and gives a good look at Black Thought's
wordplay and Danger Mouse's abstract approach to production.

The production, in my opinion, is a testament to Black Thought's rapping
ability. The beat starts, and you're like, how could someone rap on this? And
then he comes into the beat like Eileen Gu flying through moguls (does Eileen
even ski moguls?). The
[title track](https://open.spotify.com/track/6bWvTlK4QkbyYTt2SQtZHP) is a good
example of this.

**[Strangers](https://open.spotify.com/track/4H8HyHyHqUBHMg8ih2xJ1Y)** is where
the modernity is most prevalent: the beat borders on trap and the A$AP Rocky
feature has a cloud rap cadence.

**[Because](https://open.spotify.com/track/64BcZhe3utI4UhRx4IueSU)** is an
awesome posse cut, but
**[Belize](https://open.spotify.com/track/7e8OtRUdgLwSSLi00bf6Zk)** is the
standout track for me. It's Danger Mouse at his very best, creating an eerie
atmosphere with the lightly cascading piano and minor key bass line. Black
Thought's rhyming is mesmerizing, then DOOM's is transcendent. In typical MF DOOM fashion, you gotta read
the lyrics to see he's not actually rhyming every single word.

Sweet album cover too. Hopefully it evens out the average in this week's issue.
</Album>
