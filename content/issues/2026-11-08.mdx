This week I wasn't expecting to fall in love with a Malian desert blues album,
but here we are.

A few years ago I discovered the book *1001 Albums You Must Hear Before You
Die*, along with an incredible [website](https://1001albumsgenerator.com)
someone made that basically gives you one of those albums at random to listen
to daily. I love dedicating a full day to one new album on repeat, but it's
tough to keep up with! This week I did a little catch-up, and I was instantly
mesmerized by [Ali Farka Touré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Farka_Tour%C3%A9).

From all accounts, Ali seemed to live a rich and radiant life, and it is
infectious in his music. He was born in Mali, the last of 10 children, but he
was the only child to survive infancy. He was born into a more "noble" caste in
Mali, one that was higher up than the musical performers of the lower caste. He
was drawn from a young age to learn guitar, but because it was forbidden, he
made his own in secret. Over the years that musical passion boiled over and
became his career.

*Savane* is Ali's final record: he approved the masters weeks before he died of
bone cancer. You would never be able to guess that from the music. It is so
energetic and alive.

Many of the instruments used throughout I was unfamiliar with. Mixing electric
guitar blues with these West African instruments, along with the repetitive,
pulsing grooves, makes it hypnotic and dazzling.

I'd point you to
**[Machengoidi](https://open.spotify.com/track/3ebX9hWOaAjdVDQyKV05ye)** first.
It previews how the electric guitar is woven into the trio of
[ngoni](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W0O_6kmUMws) and how the
[calabash](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U455OyyGzlg) drives the songs
forward. The [njarka violin](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SlY2LLDZ2Z8) is
also prominent on this
one, probably easier to pick out of the mix. This song is the most ritualistic
sounding.

**[Beto](https://open.spotify.com/track/1ijw06hPpZhswfodIGgC1A)** is much more
blues sounding. It brings in a saxophone and female backing vocals that play
well with Ali's weathered, dusty grandpa voice.

The title track,
**[Savane](https://open.spotify.com/track/1M4tuRq8WrDUSlFDXYfLwP)**, is a lot
slower, has lots of instrumental side quests, and is more reggae inspired. It
leaves time for the lyrical content to soak in.

Finally, the last track,
**[N'jarou](https://open.spotify.com/track/6kNvTzXr5o7gXvdpkQp4Dn)**, is what
I'd expect most of you guys to like the most (myself included). It's warm,
uplifting, and slightly more progressive.

Hypnotic is the word I couldn't use enough to describe this one. And radiant.
After listening to any song on this project I feel like I could run through a
brick wall. What a beautiful farewell from someone who humbly leveraged his
success for the benefit of his country and community. As Ali put it,
"honey is not for one mouth only."
</Album>

<Section title="Where Jazz Meets Techno">

[Squarepusher](https://open.spotify.com/artist/4mtHSXwIHihO6MWNq5Qoko) is Tom
Jenkinson, and he has the coolest
[moniker](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/moniker) in all of music. Squarepusher is an
IDM project, and it's been fairly impenetrable to me for years.

I finally "got" his debut album—

Let me back up. IDM stands for Intelligent Dance Music, and it was created as
this genre to prove that electronic dance music could be more sophisticated. So
it's basically a pretentious music lover's dream come true.

It was pioneered by [Autechre](https://open.spotify.com/artist/6WH1V41LwGDGmlPUhSZLHO)
and [Aphex Twin](https://open.spotify.com/artist/6kBDZFXuLrZgHnvmPu9NsG), among
others, and it spun out into a foundational genre pretty quickly. You can hear
its DNA in a ton of contemporary music, from artists like
[Charli XCX](https://open.spotify.com/artist/25uiPmTg16RbhZWAqwLBy5),
[Kanye](https://open.spotify.com/artist/5K4W6rqBFWDnAN6FQUkS6x), and
[Bon Iver](https://open.spotify.com/artist/4LEiUm1SRbFMgfqnQTwUbQ).

Now back to Tom Jenkinson. From a very young age, Tom attached himself to jazz
fusion and became a bass virtuoso.

Later in his teenage years, he got interested in dance music and techno. His
music career shifted towards the techno side of things, but his jazz fusion
roots stayed with him.

His Squarepusher music is hard to get into because it's a blend of jazz fusion and IDM.
*[Feed Me Weird Things](https://open.spotify.com/album/3mr4Eo5QyvLqvghU1usLM4)*
(the debut) is basically jazz fusion, chopped up, distorted in some areas,
layered onto a breakbeat. I didn't really register this was what it was for a
while.

I went into a deep 90s techno rabbit hole this week, and the next morning I
just wanted to dissect this album.

<Album
  artist="Squarepusher"
  title="Ultravisitor"
  year={2004}
  cover="/covers/squarepusher-ultravisitor.jpg"
  href="https://open.spotify.com/album/2DgDJIt55XlfyHpep3CFKs"
  pills={["idm", "nu jazz", "jazz fusion"]}
>
It's a really good culmination of his career up to this point. I was on the
edge of my seat as it goes back and forth between IDM and jazz fusion.

You can't really tell how any of it was made. Some of the recordings are live
or semi-live, and you never can totally tell if an instrument was programmed or
played live.

The opener and title track,
**[Ultravisitor](https://open.spotify.com/track/3ooPG9c7MgS7bBedpNV8t6)**, is
techno, melodic, and maybe live (it starts with clapping)? It grows to this
organ/piano outro that reverbs itself into
**[I Fulcrum](https://open.spotify.com/track/6s090Bc3k6PhL3x5cf0uby)**.

**[Iambic 9 Poetry](https://open.spotify.com/track/7v0X5s4VKoilJWp7iapIr9)** is
by far the easiest one to point to. It's emotional, cinematic, with live
drumming, and slowly swells without exploding into chaos. It's followed by
**[Andrei](https://open.spotify.com/track/6QsA4nB0q6hZsDtaGUNBMP)**, which
sounds like a withdrawn Spanish guitar song.

From there, it actually does explode into chaos.
**[50 Cycles](https://open.spotify.com/track/1qooFcznYtVvXS9mM9IXoY)** is like
that part in a scary movie when you can sense a jumpscare is about to happen,
you just don't know when. I would say it's the calm before the storm, but it's
not calm at all.

**[Steinbolt](https://open.spotify.com/track/0iQCEbP69CWii57erwJO37)** is my
standout in the chaos. It sounds like
[the band from *Mad Max: Fury Road*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z4_SJfCGQwM) being
live streamed over a weak Wi-Fi connection. If you need to absolutely lock in,
this is the song.

Anyways, it does find its way out of the chaos eventually.
**[Tommib Help Buss](https://open.spotify.com/track/5RQjC6zC0Jj166iNydg654)**
could easily be used in a movie scene at sunset, and the Spanish guitar is back
in **[Every Day I Love](https://open.spotify.com/track/61NDioEKz76T6lGdmSBgSj)**.

Jazz music and techno/dance music don't sound like they should ever work
together, but Squarepusher really has made it happen. He's in a league of his
own.
</Album>
