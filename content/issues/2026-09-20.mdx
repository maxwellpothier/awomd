Two years ago, I would have told you Gen Z was in a music drought. A generation
that might lose all good music production because of TikTok and "the
algorithm." One year ago, I would have said "there's hope," after seeing acts
emerging like Wednesday, Geese, and feeble little horse (to name a few).

Now, I'm all in on Gen Z. In fact, it's the majority of music that is exciting
me right now.

Putting that in the concrete this week is Lucy Bedroque, a new digicore/rage
artist from LA (a Gen Z rapper). I have loved seeing the evolution and really
creative output of slayr (similar genre), but seeing Lucy take that same,
heavily Playboi Carti and SOPHIE inspired hip hop and give it a full other
dimension on *unmusique* blew me away.

I'd point you to **2010 Justin Bieber** as a starting point, with weirdly
beautiful, high pitched and extremely animated vocals. My personal fav is
probably **GG Anthem**, with more of an EDM drive to it. Prepare for extreme
overstimulation.

Lucy's been in this "rage" lane for a while, but took a 180° detour in 2024 on
an EP where they released their most popular song on streaming, **How To
Pretend**. This one is totally different: sounds like an internet fairy came to
earth, heard a lot of Imogen Heap and a little reggaeton, went back into the
internet and tried to create an "earth" song.

Then a day after all this they release a new album, *C*, where I can't help but
notice Richard James in writing creds?

Anyway, these Gen Z maximalist hip hop artists are masterful producers. I place
a very high value on self production, and on *unmusique* Lucy exceeds that bar.

This rabbit hole also led me back to a song from earlier this year I initially
dismissed.

First track to point you to is **Blood**, which is a killer riff into a melody
that stretches and pulls you in and pushes you out like you're floating in the
ocean.

If you handle that, try **Mouse**, which is much more exploratory and intimate.
Probably my personal fav. Gorgeous arrangements with dense lyrics that can be
seen as very fun or very deep.

Finally, **Crash Landing** has a durable rhythm section.
</Album>
