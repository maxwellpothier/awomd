I've got a weird parasocial relationship with
[Cameron Winter](https://open.spotify.com/artist/0kGweFvHWUfh6oLnookVeO). It's
honestly really uncomfortable to me and a bit awkward to write about.

It started with getting into
[Geese](https://open.spotify.com/artist/0WCo84qtCKfbyIf1lqQWB4) when they were
opening for [Spoon](https://open.spotify.com/artist/0K1q0nXQ8is36PzOKAMbNe) back
in 2022. I was supposed to go to the concert, and I was excited to see the
opener who was getting a ton of buzz at that time, albeit that buzz was fairly
niche at the time.

I ended up having a conflict with the concert and didn't go. Lame.

But something about Geese's music stuck with me.

Their breakout album
*[3D Country](https://open.spotify.com/album/4RT2pNX9ZXCB0LtqfI2sdJ)* came out
the following year. The singles were weird, country-infused, bluesy rock songs
that I wasn't quite sure I would get into.

Then **[2122](https://open.spotify.com/track/2XUQ0AYl4HPE0oK0qt70qw)** came out
with the album as the opening track and it all clicked for me. It's still got
the blues influence, but they lean so far forward into how wacky they are as a
band it's one of those things where you go "oh, *this* is what they're going
for". If you're looking for an unhinged rock song, this is the one.

Fast forward to December 2024. I put a few 3D Country songs in my
Spotify Wrapped top 10, and I get a notification that Cameron Winter dropped a
solo project called
*[Heavy Metal](https://open.spotify.com/album/7mOrnQqDad3RgYQsJGaaqk)*.
Interesting.

Didn't click for me at first, but then we get to March 2025 and I randomly
listen to **[Love Takes Miles](https://open.spotify.com/track/2zf1izCOz2F22PF27uhxRF)**
again and read the lyrics. Are you kidding me? This is one of my favorite songs
ever! It clicked.

My daughter (almost 2 at the time) really liked listening to a song together
before I put her to bed (I really liked listening to a song together before I
put her to bed). That night I tried Love Takes Miles. She caught a glimpse of the
album cover, grabbed my phone, stared at the album cover and let the song repeat
4 times. I kid you not that her eyes did not leave that screen (probably great
for a 2 year old right before bed).

From then on, she was a fan. We listened to Love Takes Miles on
repeat every time we were in the car for basically the entirety of 2025, and
she literally had all the lyrics memorized (or should I say all the sounds).
It won Pitchfork's song of the year and then Cam and Geese blew up.

Anyways, sorry, that was a lot. I guess this is me saying his music really
bonded my daughter and me in a deep way, and it will always remind me deeply of that
2 year old year.

Geez, let me wrap this up. Last weekend Cameron dropped his *Live at Carnegie
Hall* album, recorded when he was just 23. Extremely significant because who
was the other solo artist who performed at Carnegie Hall in that age range with
just them and their instrument on stage?
[Bob Dylan](https://open.spotify.com/artist/74ASZWbe4lXaubB36ztrGX).

The Dylan comps have been flying since Winter's debut came out, and this just
made those more real. It's semi-blasphemous, but I get it. Both are insanely
talented lyricists, are in the
[Americana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americana_(music)) spectrum, and have
a voice that makes you scratch your head on first listen.

I wrote a bit about **[It All Fell in the River](https://open.spotify.com/track/6QoOy0hBf3GnUyZiEVCHol)**
last week, and I think it's the most accessible song here. It's also one of
the new songs, not on his studio album, so the piano version is the only one
I've heard.

I love the piano version of these songs, but there are a few on here I have a
hard time pointing you to over the studio album version.
**[Love Takes Miles](https://open.spotify.com/track/2zf1izCOz2F22PF27uhxRF)**,
with its unique production and space for Cam's voice to breathe,
**[Cancer of the Skull](https://open.spotify.com/track/1RkuN09alt0DLZ9PlplUcc)**,
whose emotional weight is more real on the studio album, and
**[The Rolling Stones](https://open.spotify.com/track/7sMZ7doYb71bHK8tqviTCu)**,
which is my current favorite song of all time, and has been for over a year.
Sue me.

There are a couple other songs on here I'm just hearing for the first time, and
like all the Cameron Winter songs, it's been so fun diving into the lyrics.

**[Emperor XIII in Shades](https://open.spotify.com/track/6UIqRlYsnQVpWzCD1GkoeX)**,
as I read into it, is all about how we live and enjoy our days with a total
ignorance to what made them possible.

> It was a long day for Jesus\
> And a quiet night in California

**[If You Turn Back Now](https://open.spotify.com/track/0v0BMYE3RHQI8vANQRCGd6)**
has been harder to know the bigger meaning of, but I really ponder this part:

> ...the devil is real and he's living in Heaven\
> ...but I belong to Him who gave me away
</Album>

He has this weird way of tying religious themes into his music that sound
almost ironic, but I think that's the point. Diving into religious beliefs is
hard, uncomfortable, and vulnerable. You can hear this religious theme most
clearly on **[$0](https://open.spotify.com/track/2zDeZ7u3xsilDm86u1bDBf)**.

> God is real, God is real\
> I'm not kidding, God is actually real\
> I'm not kidding this time\
> I think God is actually for real\
> God is real, God is actually real\
> God is real, I wouldn't joke about this\
> I'm not kidding this time

Anyways, TLDR here is Cameron's music is deeply nostalgic for me, reminds me of
the time when my daughter called anything related to him "Cam-Aid", and his
explosion in popularity recently makes that parasocial relationship even weirder.

<Section title="Slacker Rap">

I've got a theory where if there's anyone critically acclaimed, even if you're
not their biggest fan, you should go to their show. Lots of times they put on a
show that makes you appreciate their music in a new light.

That is what happened with me and
[Earl Sweatshirt](https://open.spotify.com/artist/3A5tHz1SfngyOZM2gItYKu) when I
saw him 2 weeks ago.

He's been around for a long time now, and has a similar arc to
[Tyler, the Creator](https://open.spotify.com/artist/4V8LLVI7PbaPR0K2TGSxFF).
They both started with this deeply immature
"[horror rap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horrorcore)" type of music (both as
part of the same [Odd Future](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odd_Future)
collective), and have evolved into making
[conscious hip hop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conscious_hip_hop). Earl's
[debut](https://open.spotify.com/album/5vRfIDOPJHy3W2wHWbzLlE) always reminds me
of when I made the switch from Pandora to Spotify, where I was like "wow, I can
listen to **[Chum](https://open.spotify.com/track/6plT7nFGiXKSBP9HFSI4ef)**
whenever I want?" He's been doing this for a long time.

His concert made me realize his music has evolved into his phase of fatherhood
now, so I connected with it more deeply.

<Album
  artist="Earl Sweatshirt"
  title="Live Laugh Love"
  year={2025}
  cover="/covers/earl-sweatshirt-live-laugh-love.jpg"
  href="https://open.spotify.com/album/45aldsXQsGrQxpst6z2hfR"
  pills={["abstract hip hop", "experimental hip hop"]}
>
This is his fatherhood album, as you can kinda tell by the cover and title.

It's full of off-kilter beats that sound extra trippy because Earl has this way
of rapping where his voice is slightly behind the beat, almost like he's
chasing it.

The project is 24 minutes, with an average song length of about 2 minutes, in
classic Earl fashion.

I love **[Gamma (need the \<3)](https://open.spotify.com/track/3KH2Gs1noFVM6AELjq03vc)**
and **[Live](https://open.spotify.com/track/0TR79tPMeiaDRxnSjvKYh3)**, mainly
for the production. They both layer on instruments that are "off" for the
majority of each musical measure, but come together at the end of each
measure. **[TOURMALINE](https://open.spotify.com/track/7sam5WsFimXgFOCuEOc90x)**
is the most lyrically focused on his transition to being a family man, and I
really like it as well.

His own dad died in 2018, who he had a complicated relationship with, so he brings
a lot of that into this album. It's a cool transition from the dark place he
was in for a long time after his passing to his own independence and deciding
how he was going to move forward.
</Album>
