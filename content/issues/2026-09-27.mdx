Two years ago, I would have told you Gen Z was in a music drought. A generation
that might lose good music production because TikTok and the algorithm
are so focused on a quick dopamine hit.

One year ago, I would have said "there's hope," after seeing acts
emerging like [Wednesday](https://open.spotify.com/artist/4j7DrazfBZLLD0OrVoAtEe),
[Geese](https://open.spotify.com/artist/0WCo84qtCKfbyIf1lqQWB4), and
[feeble little horse](https://open.spotify.com/artist/2GJa7lPCjAB1rKXptXrfy8) (to
name a few).

Now, I'm all in on Gen Z. In fact, it's the majority of music that is exciting
me right now.

Putting that in the concrete this week is
[Lucy Bedroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucy_Bedroque), a new
[digicore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digicore)/[rage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rage_(music_genre))
artist from LA (a Gen Z rapper).

I have loved seeing the evolution and really
creative output of [slayr](https://open.spotify.com/artist/0SL3lfuoRP67mrvkdnwIMA) (similar
genre), but seeing Lucy take that same, heavily
[Playboi Carti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Playboi_Carti) and
[SOPHIE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sophie_(musician)) inspired hip hop and give it a full other
dimension on *unmusique* blew me away.

I'd point you to **[2010 Justin Bieber](https://open.spotify.com/track/5WIGbrfNMvFeCQOx5XykBO)** as a starting point, with weirdly
beautiful, high pitched and extremely animated vocals. My personal fav is
probably **[G6 Anthem](https://open.spotify.com/track/0rq8Vr1Rwjgf97tAcT9EkN)**, with more of an EDM drive to it. Prepare for extreme
overstimulation.

Lucy's been in this "rage" lane for a while, but took a 180° detour in 2024 on
an EP where they released their most popular song on streaming, **[How to Pretend](https://open.spotify.com/track/4IKG0oDjlYYJKSGhPxaoSd)**. This one is totally different: sounds like an internet fairy came to
earth, heard a lot of [Imogen Heap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imogen_Heap) and a little [reggaeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reggaeton), went back into the
internet and tried to create an "earth" song.

Then a day after all this they release a new album,
*[c](https://open.spotify.com/album/6cwZdsIawCaF4Ukh7KV24l)*, where I can't help
but notice [Richard D. James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aphex_Twin) in
writing creds?

Anyway, these Gen Z maximalist hip hop artists are masterful producers. I place
a very high value on self production, and on *Unmusique* Lucy blows my mind with
what they're able to do with fairly minimal production help.

It's not all explicit, aggressive hip hop this week, because I also have to
talk about...

<Album
  artist="mary in the junkyard"
  title="Role Model Hermit"
  year={2026}
  cover="/covers/mary-in-the-junkyard-role-model-hermit.jpg"
  href="https://open.spotify.com/album/0r5nmjIvD8FmcgWsILF1Eh"
  pills={["indie rock", "art rock", "midwest emo"]}
>
This is my current pick for album of the year.

I saw this labeled [midwest emo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midwest_emo) somewhere, which made me a little hesitant.
While I do hear a little of that sound, what pulled me in was her gorgeous voice. There's
something really compelling about hearing that voice sing such mysterious lyrics.

I also watched a few [interviews](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DfH3Bnl71Y4) with the band. They're just the most
down-to-earth people. It's endearing to see them juggle normal lives and mundane
day jobs while trying to accelerate their music career. The lead singer tells a
story about literally shucking oysters all day on release day, then playing a
show that night.

The first track to point you to is **[Blood](https://open.spotify.com/track/5qKD8WTc5qIZW96rIcgzXE)**, which stretches a killer riff into a song
that pulls you and pushes you out like you're floating in the ocean.

If you handle that, try **[Mouse](https://open.spotify.com/track/1UYBj9b4q0yXa6TChmbt0B)**, which is much more exploratory and intimate.
Probably my personal fav. Gorgeous arrangements with dense lyrics that can be
seen as very silly or very deep.

Finally, **[Crash Landing](https://open.spotify.com/track/0zeRsscPdy6177ENtSRdqj)** has a durable rhythm section that I could tolerate going on for hours.
</Album>
